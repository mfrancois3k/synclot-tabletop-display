Five changes to the deck

  1. Raise the front of the box about 75 mm (3″). With the monitor face up, anyone whose eyes are higher than the top of the reflector loses the top of the picture: about 13% for someone seated 2 m away and about 30% at 1 m. Tilting the box back 12° fixes it for seated people. Someone standing close will still see mostly lid. Calculated
  2. Cut the reflector to 19½ × 12″. A 24″ sheet doesn't fit a 22″ bag, and the box only needs 19½″. Calculated
  3. Build the box from 5 mm foam board. In ¼″ plywood the kit comes to about 6.4 kg, over your 4.5 kg limit. In foam board it's about 3.8 kg with the tube. Estimate
  4. Draw the box page upside down. The reflection flips it top to bottom. One line of CSS, plus a test card to confirm. Calculated
  5. Make each screen its own page on one local link. If your laptop can't drive two screens, any second device with HDMI can run the tube.

Two things you should also know. The tube is readable from 2 m for one short line only (for example "ROW B"); the rest is for the person sitting in front of it. And your hologram plan says not to build the tube before the pilot locks on December 13.

Risks first

1. Brightness with the lights on

Numbers
The monitor is 300 nits Published. Two-way mirror should return roughly 60 to 70% of that, so white lands near 180 to 210 nits and a pure red stall near 40 Estimate. Clear acrylic returns far less, and how much depends on which way the monitor's light is polarised: anywhere from about 1% to 18% Calculated. So clear acrylic can look fine one way round and almost vanish when the monitor is turned 90°.
Tube
The AMOLED tops out at 310 nits Published and Canal's mirror lets 25% through Published, so the car is about 78 nits at best Calculated. A bright shirt reflected in the mirror can match that. The tube is the piece most likely to wash out.
Cheapest test
Free tonight: picture-frame glass at 45° over your laptop, lights on, sitting down. Then the $12.50 mirror over your phone for the tube, and the $21 mirror sample against clear acrylic for the box. With clear acrylic, try the screen both ways round.
Pass
A stranger names the building and finds the red stall in about 3 seconds from 2 m, lights on.

2. Does the AMOLED fit the tube?

Numbers
The screen itself should fit. It is 4.8 mm thick, or 11 mm with its HDMI holder Published, and a board up to about 90 mm wide clears a 95 mm tube when it sits near the middle Calculated. The cables are the real question: I couldn't confirm which edge the HDMI and USB sockets are on, or the board's exact outline Unknown. A plug sticking out sideways would not fit.
Cheapest test
Free: cut a 95 mm (3¾″) hole in cardboard. When the screen arrives, plug both cables in and pass it through the hole. Do this before ordering the $78 tube.
Pass
Screen and plugged-in cables pass through without bending a plug. If not: right-angle adapters, then a 6″ tube.

3. Can one laptop drive both screens?

Numbers
It depends on the laptop, and I don't know yours Unknown. Some thin laptops run only one outside screen; several MacBook Air models are like that (check Apple's page for your exact model). The laptop also needs one USB-C port that carries video, one HDMI, and two USB ports, so expect to need a small hub.
Cheapest test
Free: plug in any two screens you own (a TV and a monitor) and try to show a different window on each.
Pass
Three different pictures: laptop, screen A, screen B. If it fails, run the tube page from a second device with HDMI. The design below already allows for that.

4. The top of the picture gets cut off (found while drawing)

Numbers
The reflector's top edge is only about 23 cm above the table. Seated eyes are about 45 cm above it. Looking down over that edge hides the top of the picture Calculated.
Cheapest test
Same picture-frame test, sitting at normal height 2 m away. Then prop the laptop's front edge up 3″ and look again.
Pass
The whole test card shows from every seat. If the tilt looks odd, the other fix is the monitor in the lid facing down; that is a different box.

Wiring

  • Monitor: one USB-C cable carries picture and power Published, if the laptop's port supports video.
  • AMOLED: draws up to 650 mA at 5 V on a white screen Published. That is more than an old-style USB port gives, so use a blue USB 3 port or a 5 V 2 A wall plug.
  • LEDs: four pixels need at most about 240 mA, so the controller's 5V pin can feed them Calculated. The controller signals at 3.3 V into a 5 V strip. That usually works on a short wire; if the LEDs flicker, add a 74AHCT125 level shifter Estimate.
  • LED behaviour: start with a steady soft white glow that needs no link to the laptop. Never red: red is reserved for the found stall.

What each screen shows

  • Box page: pure black, no buttons. W62 left, W57 right, each with its floor ladder. On a find, the found building takes 60% of the width, the other drops to about a third brightness, the floor slides forward and one stall turns red.
  • Sizes for 2 m: labels at least 80 px tall (16 mm on this monitor) and the red stall at least 115 px wide (23 mm) Calculated.
  • Flip: the page is drawn with transform: scaleY(-1). Left and right stay as they are. Keep the top 10% black as a margin.
  • Test card: press T for a card with the word TOP, "W62 left", a border at the screen edge and one red square. Press V or H to flip if it reads wrong.
  • Tube page: the car, one large line (160 px, readable at 2 m), then building, floor and "last seen" for the person in front. "Last seen" is the time Sync Lot last recorded the car. It is not live tracking.
  • Stall or row: the box lights a stall, as in your deck. Your app rule says porters see a row. The message carries both, so either screen can show either.

Phone-to-laptop message

A small relay runs on the laptop. The phone, the box page and the tube page each connect to it over a local WebSocket and say who they are. The phone sends one short JSON message per tap.

// phone → relay → both screens
{ "v": 1, "type": "find", "seq": 42,
  "building": "W62", "floor": "B2",
  "place": "B-06", "row": "B",
  "car": { "id": "6062641", "label": "X5 · Carbon Black" },
  "lastSeen": "2026-11-11T07:42:00-05:00" }

{ "v": 1, "type": "show",  "seq": 43, "building": "W57", "floor": "L2" }
{ "v": 1, "type": "clear", "seq": 44 }

// relay → phone, after every message
{ "v": 1, "type": "state", "ack": 42,
  "showing": { "building": "W62", "floor": "B2", "place": "B-06" },
  "screens": { "box": true, "tube": false } }

// first message from every client
{ "v": 1, "type": "hello", "role": "remote" }   // or "box", "tube"
  • The highest seq wins. A new message interrupts the running animation from where it is; nothing queues up.
  • If no state comes back within a second, the phone says "Box not connected" and carries on as the normal app. On reconnect it resends its last message.
  • An unknown building, floor or place lights nothing and returns "type": "error". The box never shows a red stall it can't place.
  • Use your phone's hotspot with the laptop joined to it. Guest Wi-Fi in offices often blocks one device from reaching another Estimate.

Build order

Free tests come first. Stop at any fail.

  1. Picture-frame test, free. Glass at 45° over the laptop, lights on, seated 2 m away, then with the front propped up 3″. Pass: the whole test card shows and TOP reads the right way after the flip.
  2. Two-screen test, free. Any two screens on your laptop. Pass: a different window on each. Fail: plan a second device for the tube.
  3. Pages and phone link, free. Build both pages and the relay; test on any screen over your hotspot. Pass: switch buildings 10 times fast and the last tap always wins. Turn the hotspot off and on: the phone says so, then recovers.
  4. Mirror samples, about $34. $12.50 Canal piece over your phone; $21 sample against clear acrylic. Pass: the red square reads from 2 m with the lights on.
  5. Monitor and ugly rig, $139.99. Monitor flat, sheet on books at 45°, split view. Pass: a stranger names the building and the red stall in about 3 seconds. Fail: go to the fallbacks.
  6. Cut and assemble the box. Pass: the reflector's top edge is as high above the monitor as it is forward of its bottom edge (that is 45°), nothing wobbles, and it comes apart flat.
  7. Cardboard-hole test, then the tube. Only after December 13, and only after the screen and its cables pass through a 95 mm hole. Pass: no screen edge visible; the car reads as floating from 1 m in front with the lights on.
  8. Wire the LEDs. Pass: a steady glow for 10 minutes with the screen on full white, no flicker, no visible dots.
  9. Pack and time it. Pass: one bag, 4.5 kg or less on a luggage scale, set up in 10 minutes without tools.
  10. Dress rehearsal. Three finds in each building, then pull the hotspot mid-demo. Pass: every find lands in the right building and the link recovers without touching the laptop.

Fallbacks

  1. Building toggle. Same box, same page, one building full screen. Send show to switch. The red stall doubles in size. The room no longer sees both buildings at once.
  2. Phone AR over a printed plan. One printed plan per building, a red marker pinned on it through the phone camera. One viewer at a time. It lives in the bag as the backup that needs no box.
  3. The phone. If everything else fails, hand them the app.

Later, not designed here

  • Turning base: geared motor, bearings, angle sensor.
  • Face-tracking USB camera.
  • Single-board computer in the base, so the tube runs without the laptop.
  • Microphone, speaker and volume knob for spoken requests.
  • LEDs that react to a find (the controller already has a USB link for this).

The pitch

Swipe sideways through the four slides.

1 / 4, the problem

Runners lose cars across two buildings and 14 floors.

W 62nd St and W 57th St, seven floors each: Level 4, Level 3, Level 2, Low Level, B1, B2 and B3. A car logged in the wrong building or on the wrong floor means walking floor after floor.

Every wrong guess means a customer kept waiting and a runner off the queue.

2 / 4, the solution

Sync Lot: search the car and it shows you the building, the floor and the stall.

Type or say the car. The app picks W62 or W57, shows the floor, lights the stall in red and gives the runner one Up Next card. The dispatcher moves cars, and the map updates for everyone.

The app is what you're selling. Everything below is how you show it in a room.

3 / 4, why a display

The display is for the room. It isn't the product.

A phone screen is too small for a table of five people. A physical display shows both buildings at once, so everyone watches the search land in the right one, from across the table.

If the display fails, the pitch still works: you hand them the phone.

4 / 4, the one rule

Red means found. Everything else stays quiet.

Dark floors, dark cars, no neon. When you tap Find Your Car, one stall turns red. That's the moment the room remembers.

Whatever display you build, it has to make that red stall readable from 2 metres away.

The build in phases

Phase 1 of 6
The 3D view didn't load on this device. Each phase's checklist is below.
Phase 1, gather parts

Get only what the free test can't answer.

  1. Order the 17.3" Arzopa A1M ($139.99).
  2. Order the $21 two-way mirror test piece first. Buy the 12×24" sheet after the test.
  3. Get black foam board or plywood for a box about 20×14×14", black velvet and two rails.
  4. Get the USB-C or HDMI cable your laptop needs, and a flat padded bag about 22×16".

Go when the monitor shows your laptop over one cable and the panels fit flat in the bag.

Phase 2, the ugly test rig

Books, a sheet of acrylic and the monitor on a table.

  1. Dark room, monitor flat and face up.
  2. Prop the sheet on books at 45°, top edge toward you.
  3. Play the split view: W62 left, W57 right, one red stall.
  4. Swap clear acrylic for two-way mirror and compare.

Go if a stranger names the building and finds the red stall in about 3 seconds from 2 metres, lights on. No-go: try the building toggle, then the AR window.

Phase 3, frame and case

Build the black box around it.

  1. Cut a base, back, two sides and a lid: about 20" wide, 14" deep, 14" tall.
  2. Use slot joints or tape hinges so it builds with no tools.
  3. Line the inside with black velvet. Fix the rails at 45° and check with a square.
  4. Slide in the reflector, top edge at the front.

Go when the reflector sits at 45° without wobbling, no room light hits the screen, and it folds flat again.

Phase 4, electronics and software link

Phone picks, laptop draws, box shows.

  1. Build the display page: both buildings on black, W62 left, W57 right, no buttons.
  2. Have the phone send the building, floor and stall, for example W62, B2, B-06. A local WebSocket will likely feel quicker than a cloud round trip.
  3. Make the found building grow to about 60% of the screen and dim the other.
  4. Laptop to monitor, full screen, camera tilted slightly from above.

Go if tap-to-red feels live and the right building lights every time. Switch buildings 10 times fast; aim for about 150 ms (a rule of thumb, not a measured figure).

Phase 5, finish and hide the electronics

Nothing but the two floating buildings.

  1. Add the front lip over the monitor's edge.
  2. Run the one cable through a hole in the back.
  3. Move the laptop behind the box.
  4. Optional: set the W62 and W57 name plates under each half.

Go when nobody can see a cable, a screen edge or the laptop from the front.

Phase 6, demo day

Pack flat, unpack in about 10 minutes (est.).

  1. Panels, monitor and reflector (in its film) go flat into one padded bag.
  2. Unpack: base, walls, lid, rails, reflector, monitor, cable.
  3. Laptop on, display page full screen, phone connected.
  4. Run Find Your Car three times, in both buildings, before anyone sits down.

Go when a search lands in each building. Time your setup at home first. Backup: the app on your phone and the two AR mats.
